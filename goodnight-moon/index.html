Goodnight Moon

Originally by Margaret Wise Brown — Retold for grown-ups
🕐 8 min read 📖 783 words 🌙 Best read aloud

There's a room you remember.

Maybe not this exact one, but one like it. Warm light. Soft corners. The kind of room where the world outside stopped mattering for a while.

In this room there was a fireplace. Not a big one — just enough to pop and hum to itself. A fire that had been going so long it didn't need to prove anything anymore.

There was a red balloon tied to the bedpost. Nobody remembers who put it there. It just drifted, turning slowly in the warm air, like it was thinking about something.

A picture on the wall of a cow jumping over the moon. You know cows can't do that. But whoever painted it believed they could, and honestly, that's enough.

Next to it — three little bears sitting in chairs. Papa reading the paper. Mama knitting something shapeless. Baby just sitting there with that look baby bears have. The one that says I know something and I'm not telling.

Two kittens on the rug. One batting at dust in the firelight. The other curled so tight it was basically a circle. Neither of them doing anything useful. Kittens never do.

Mittens on the hearth, drying. Red wool, stretched at the thumbs. They smelled like snow and something you can't name but know right away. Something like home.

A little mouse lived behind the wall. She had a matchbox bed and a thimble she used for soup. She had everything she needed, which wasn't much. Mice understand something it takes people forever to learn — enough isn't a limit. It's a kind of freedom.

She came out every evening to sit by the fire. The kittens never chased her. Some deal had been made between them long ago. This was the hour when everything was warm and winding down, and there was no room in it for chasing.

And there was a quiet old lady in the rocking chair. Silver hair, still hands. She didn't read or knit. She just rocked, slowly, and whispered.

Hush.

Not to anyone. To the room itself. To the evening.

The room listened.

Goodnight room.

Goodnight to the red balloon, still turning. Still thinking its slow thoughts.

Goodnight moon. The real one, in the window. Full tonight, hanging low like a lantern someone left on for you.

Goodnight cow jumping over the moon. You impossible, wonderful cow.

Goodnight light. And the red balloon.

The fire's lower now. Just embers pulsing like a heartbeat.

Goodnight bears. Goodnight chairs. Papa's done with his paper. Mama's knitting grew another inch. Baby still isn't telling.

Goodnight kittens. Goodnight mittens. One kitten gave up on the dust. The other hasn't moved. The mittens are warm all the way through now. Tomorrow someone will pull them on again. But that's tomorrow.

Goodnight clocks. Goodnight socks. There's a clock on the mantle ticking so softly you can only hear it when everything else goes quiet. And the socks — thick wool ones draped on a chair. The kind that make you feel like you've got your life together, even when you don't.

Goodnight little house. Goodnight mouse. She's heading back behind the wall now. Matchbox bed. Cotton-ball pillow. A thimble of lentil soup. Her enough.

Goodnight nobody. This one's important. Goodnight to the empty space, the open door, the absence that sometimes sits in the corner. You don't have to make it leave. Just say goodnight, and it gets a little smaller.

Goodnight mush. The bowl on the nightstand. Just oats and milk. The food you eat when the day's been too much and all you need is something warm that asks nothing of you.

Goodnight to the old lady whispering hush. She's still rocking. Slower now. The whisper's become just breath.

Hush.

Goodnight stars. Look up. Too many of them. Nobody needs that many stars. But there they are — ancient, excessive, completely unbothered by your problems. And somehow that's the most comforting thing in the world.

Goodnight air. Goodnight to the breath you're taking right now. You don't have to think about it. Your body knows what to do.

Goodnight noises everywhere. The fridge hum. The house settling. A dog barking two streets over. Your own pulse, if you listen.

These are the sounds of your life. Not glamorous. But yours.

The fire's out. The balloon stopped turning. The kittens are asleep. The old lady's eyes are closed.

The moon's still there in the window. It'll be there when you wake up, or it won't. But right now it's here.

Right now, everything is here.

You.

Goodnight.

Hush.
