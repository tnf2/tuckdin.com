The Goose That Laid the Golden Eggs

Originally by Aesop — Retold for grown-ups
🕐 7 min read 📖 781 words 🌙 Best read aloud

The farmer found the egg on a Tuesday.

Almost missed it. Morning rounds — same circuit he'd done for twenty-two years. Henhouse, goat pen, barn. He wasn't paying attention. Morning rounds aren't the kind of thing you pay attention to.

The egg was in the straw beneath the grey goose. Something caught his eye — a glint, a warmth, a color that didn't belong.

Gold. Not golden. Not yellowish. Gold. Dense, smooth, impossibly heavy.

He picked it up. The weight of it settled into his palm the way significance settles into a moment.

"Huh," said the farmer. He was not a dramatic man.

His wife looked at it. Held it to the light.

"Huh," she said. They were well-matched.

The goldsmith tested it. Weighed it. Scraped a line across the bottom. Looked up with an expression the farmer had never seen on a goldsmith before.

"It's gold."

He paid handsomely. Enough for the fence, a new roof, boots that kept water out, and a dress his wife had been looking at in the catalog for three months without mentioning.

That night they sat at the kitchen table and felt something unfamiliar: the absence of worry. Not happiness exactly — but the weight lifting. Like taking off a heavy coat you forgot you were wearing.

Next morning, another egg. Same goose, same gold. And the next morning. And the next.

Every day, one golden egg. The farmer collected them calmly, took them to the goldsmith, deposited the money. He didn't tell anyone. They'd lived quietly for twenty-two years and saw no reason to stop.

But money gets noticed. The fence got replaced. The roof went from patched to new. A second barn appeared. People talked — at the market, over fences. Where's the money coming from?

The farmer didn't mind the talk. What he minded was the waiting.

Every morning, the walk to the henhouse got longer. Not physically — same thirty steps. But inside his chest, it stretched. What if the egg wasn't there? What if the goose just... stopped?

Once you build your life around a miracle, the miracle becomes a requirement. A gift makes you grateful. A requirement makes you anxious.

He started waking at three. Then two. Lying there thinking about the egg, the goose, the gold. His wife noticed.

"You're different."

"I'm fine."

"You spend more time staring at that goose than talking to me."

"That goose is the reason we have any of this."

"We had a life before the goose."

She was right. A harder life, but theirs. And he hadn't spent any of it awake at two in the morning, afraid.

The idea came quietly, dressed in logic.

If the goose laid one golden egg a day, then the gold was inside the goose. All of it. Why wait? Why take it one piece at a time when you could have it all at once?

He didn't tell his wife. Bad ideas prefer to be alone.

He went early, before dawn. The goose was sleeping. He stood over her a long time.

He did it quickly.

Inside — nothing. No gold. No hidden vault. Just a goose. Blood and bone and the warm machinery of a living thing that was no longer living.

He stood there holding what he'd done, and the silence was the loudest thing he'd ever heard.

His wife found him an hour later. She looked at the goose. She looked at him. She didn't say anything for a long time.

She didn't need to.

"Why?" she finally said.

"I wanted it all at once."

His voice broke on all, because he heard how small it sounded. All at once. As if the daily miracle — the egg in the straw, the walk to the goldsmith, the slow, steady life getting better — as if that wasn't enough.

The money lasted a while. Then it didn't. They were back to patched roofs and leaking boots. Harder this time, because they knew what they'd lost.

His wife forgave him. Slowly. One day at a time. Which was — he realized too late — exactly how the gold had come.

One day at a time. That's how everything good comes.

Goodnight. And if there's something in your life giving you one golden egg a day — a person, a job, a quiet happiness that shows up without drama —

Don't cut it open. Don't try to get it all at once.

Just say thank you. Collect the egg. Go home.

Tomorrow there'll be another one.

Goodnight.
